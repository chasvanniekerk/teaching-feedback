SPARKS · Development instrument

Learning Preferences & Learning Situations

Learn the six approaches first. Then explore which approaches you would choose in different learning situations. The aim is reflection: preferences may change with what you are trying to learn.

Private by design · answers stay only in this page
Part 1 · Short training

Six ways you might choose to learn

These are approaches you can choose—not fixed learner types. Read them now because the same six choices will appear throughout the assessment.

Five learning situations

What you prefer may depend on what you are trying to accomplish. The assessment therefore presents five different kinds of learning situation.

How you will answer
Select every approach you would genuinely use. You may rank selected approaches when one is preferred over another, but ranking is optional and equal ranks are allowed. An unselected approach simply means “I would not choose this here”—it is not ranked last.

Development version. SPARKS is a reflective instrument under development, not a validated psychometric scale and not a claim that students should be taught according to a fixed “learning style”.

Part 2 · Practice

Try the response method

This practice item is not scored. The six approaches are exactly the same as in the assessment.

Practice item

You need to understand a new topic well enough to explain its main idea tomorrow.

Select what you would use. Ranking is optional.

Tip: selecting A and B does not require you to decide which is “better”. If they are equal, leave both unranked or give them the same rank.

20 questions

Which approaches would you use? Select all that apply. Ranking is optional; ties are allowed.
