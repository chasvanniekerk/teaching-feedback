Learning Preferences & Learning Situations

1. LEARNING PREFERENCES

Learning Preferences

Do You Know How You Prefer To Learn?

You probably don't approach every kind of learning in the same way. When learning something completely new, you might prefer to read about it or listen to someone explain it. When trying to solve a difficult problem, you might prefer to discuss it with someone. When applying what you have learned, you might prefer to try it yourself.

These are your learning preferences — the ways you prefer to approach learning. But your preferences may also depend on what you are trying to achieve. We call these different purposes Learning Situations.

Why Is This Useful?

Understanding your learning preferences can help you become more aware of how you approach learning. Instead of automatically studying in the same way every time, you can ask yourself: “What am I trying to learn, and how would I prefer to approach it?”

A preference tells you what you tend to choose. It does not prove that one method is always the best way for you to learn. The purpose of this page is to help you recognise your own patterns and think more deliberately about the approaches available to you.

Six Preferences For Active And Receptive Knowledge Strategies

SPARKS is a developing framework for thinking about learning preferences. It separates what you are trying to accomplish from how you prefer to approach the learning. The six preferences below describe six approaches you might choose. You may prefer different approaches in different Learning Situations.

3. LEARNING SITUATIONS

Introducing Learning Situations

WHY

Does The Situation Matter?

Knowing your preferences is a starting point. You also need to consider the learning situation: encountering something new, understanding it, applying what you know, solving a problem or using your knowledge in a different context.

Encountering A New Method

You first encounter a calculation method. You might read an explanation, listen to someone explain it or examine a diagram to build an initial knowledge base.

Using A Familiar Method

You now need to use that method. Receiving another explanation may not be enough: you may need to attempt a calculation and check your reasoning. Your preferences may stay the same while the situation calls for a different combination or sequence of approaches.

HOW

Do Situations Connect To VLR–DWK?

We developed the five Learning Situations within SPARKS to connect learning preferences with different learning demands. VLR–DWK describes approaches you can choose. The situation influences why you choose them and how you combine or sequence them.

For example, reading an introduction can help you acquire knowledge of a method. Applying that method may also require you to carry out a calculation or procedure yourself. Discussing why it works can support understanding, while testing an adapted version can help you transfer it to a new context.

Explore The Five Learning Situations

The five situations do not prescribe one correct approach. They help you make deliberate choices rather than choosing only what feels familiar or comfortable. You decide which activities to plan, which resources to use and whether to involve other people.

The questionnaire explores your choices across all five situations. Each appears in four scenarios, giving 20 questions. This helps you reflect on whether your preferred approaches change with the situation.
