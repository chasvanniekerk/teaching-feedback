Learning Preferences

Learning Preferences & Learning Situations

Do you know how you prefer to learn?

You probably don't approach every kind of learning in the same way. When learning something completely new, you might prefer to read about it or listen to someone explain it. When trying to solve a difficult problem, you might prefer to discuss it with someone. When applying what you have learned, you might prefer to try it yourself.

These are your learning preferences — the ways you prefer to approach learning. But your preferences may also depend on what you are trying to achieve. We call these different purposes Learning Situations.

Why is this useful?

Understanding your learning preferences can help you become more aware of how you approach learning. Instead of automatically studying in the same way every time, you can ask yourself: “What am I trying to learn, and how would I prefer to approach it?”

A preference tells you what you tend to choose. It does not prove that one method is always the best way for you to learn. The purpose of this page is to help you recognise your own patterns and think more deliberately about the approaches available to you.

Introducing SPARKS

Six Preferences for Active and Receptive Knowledge Strategies

SPARKS is a developing framework for thinking about learning preferences. It separates what you are trying to accomplish from how you prefer to approach the learning. The six preferences below describe six approaches you might choose. You may prefer different approaches in different Learning Situations.

Six ways you might choose to learn
