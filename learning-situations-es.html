Modelo en desarrollo

SPARKS es un modelo en desarrollo. Sus conceptos, cuestionarios y resultados se están explorando y perfeccionando. Aún no se ha validado científicamente y los resultados deben interpretarse como indicios de preferencias, no como diagnósticos ni estilos de aprendizaje fijos.

SPARKS · Rastro de evidencias

Situaciones de aprendizaje

¿Cómo describir lo que alguien intenta conseguir mediante el aprendizaje sin decidir de antemano cómo debe aprender? Esta página documenta la revisión bibliográfica que fundamenta la síntesis actual de situaciones de aprendizaje SPARKS.

1 · La pregunta de investigación

Propósito antes que estrategia

Para este trabajo, una situación de aprendizaje se define provisionalmente como una descripción del propósito de aprender que no impone una estrategia. «Necesito comprender un concepto desconocido» deja abiertas varias estrategias. «Necesito aprender mediante un debate» no: la estrategia ya está elegida.

Marcos existentes→¿Qué clasifican?→Filtro de neutralidad estratégica→Propósitos recurrentes→Síntesis actual

Un límite desde el principio

El objetivo no es forzar los marcos establecidos para que encajen en SPARKS. Los procesos cognitivos, actividades, métodos didácticos, resultados y etapas de experiencia pueden aportar información, pero no se convierten automáticamente en situaciones de aprendizaje.

“Directly useful” does not mean that an author's category has been adopted unchanged. It means the framework helps distinguish learning purposes while leaving room to examine strategy choice. The final five below are our synthesis, not categories claimed by these authors as a single taxonomy.

3 · The difficult middle

Why Practice and Consolidation stand out

Practice

Practice describes something the learner does repeatedly. It can strengthen fluency, accuracy, retrieval or skill, but it already specifies an activity. For a strategy-neutral taxonomy this makes Practice look more like a means of learning than the purpose confronting the learner.

Consolidation

Consolidation is theoretically important, particularly in Hattie and Donoghue's learning phases, but it describes the strengthening and stabilising of existing learning. As a learner-facing situation it is less homogeneous with goals such as understanding, applying or solving a problem. It remains relevant to the evidence base without currently being retained as a separate SPARKS Learning Situation.

Why exclusion matters

A concept can be important to learning without belonging in this taxonomy. The test is not “does research support this concept?” but “does this describe what the learner is trying to accomplish while leaving the choice of strategy open?”

4 · Current synthesis

Five candidate Learning Situations

After filtering the frameworks for purpose, conceptual level and strategy neutrality, five recurring learner purposes currently form the strongest working synthesis.

Acquiring

I need to learn something new.

Understanding

I need to make sense of something.

Applying

I need to use what I know.

Problem Solving

I need to resolve something where the solution is not immediately known.

Transferring

I need to use what I learned in a different situation.

Current status

These five Learning Situations are a developing SPARKS synthesis derived from comparison of established learning frameworks. They are not presented as an established or validated taxonomy. In particular, the boundaries between Applying, Problem Solving and Transferring require further theoretical and empirical testing.

5 · What the five mean

Keeping the boundaries visible

SituationLearner purposeCurrent boundary
AcquiringGain information or knowledge that is new to the learner.Does not prescribe whether it is encountered visually, by listening, reading, discussion, writing or doing.
UnderstandingMake sense of information, relationships or concepts.Concerned with meaning rather than merely exposure or recall.
ApplyingUse existing knowledge or skill to perform a task.The situation need not be meaningfully different from the context in which learning occurred.
Problem SolvingResolve a gap between a current state and a desired state when the solution is not immediately known.Defined by an unresolved problem rather than simply by using knowledge.
TransferringUse prior learning in a meaningfully different context or situation.Change of context distinguishes transfer from ordinary application.

These learner-facing definitions are working SPARKS formulations informed by the literature; they are not quotations from the source authors.

Research continues

The next question is deliberately skeptical: are Applying, Problem Solving and Transferring genuinely distinct learner situations, or are some special cases of a broader purpose? That boundary should be resolved before the Learning Situations are used to structure the SPARKS preference questionnaire.
